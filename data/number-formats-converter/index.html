FAQ

Number Formats Converter — Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Indian number system differ?

After the first thousand, digits are grouped in twos: 1,00,000 is one lakh (100 thousand) and 1,00,00,000 is one crore (10 million). Then arab (10⁹), kharab (10¹¹), neel (10¹³) and padma (10¹⁵). The international system groups in threes: thousand, million, billion, trillion.

How large a number can it handle?

Integers up to 10³⁰ (nonillion / 10 quadrillion crore) exactly, using arbitrary-precision arithmetic; decimals are kept to the digits you type. Beyond that, scientific notation is still produced.

Which languages are supported for words?

English (international and Indian styles), Hindi, Spanish, French (with the Swiss/Belgian septante–nonante variant off by default), German and Portuguese. Amounts in words follow each language’s currency phrasing — for Hindi, “रुपये … पैसे”.

Is the cheque format correct for banks?

It follows the common conventions: the amount in words, paise/cents as a number of hundredths, and the word “only” at the end (switchable). Always check your bank’s guidance — some ask for “Rupees” first, which the output includes.

Can it convert words back to numbers?

Yes, for English in both systems: “three crore twelve lakh”, “two million five hundred thousand” and mixed forms such as “1.5 lakh” are parsed. Other languages are output-only for now.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. Parsing and converting happen in JavaScript inside your browser tab. Your text and files are never sent to Toolity or anyone else.
