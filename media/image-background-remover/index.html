How It Works

How to Remove an Image Background in 3 Steps

1

Drop a photo

Drag a JPG, PNG or WebP onto the left pane. Portraits work best with the default model; switch to Any object in Settings for products, pets or logos.

2

Remove the background

Click Remove background. The first run downloads the AI model once (it is cached by your browser); after that it takes a few seconds per image, faster on devices with WebGPU.

3

Compare and download

Drag the slider over the result to compare with the original, pick a fill colour if you need one, then download a transparent PNG or copy it straight to the clipboard.

FAQ

Image Background Remover — Frequently Asked Questions

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The neural network runs inside your browser with Transformers.js — on your graphics card via WebGPU where available, otherwise in WebAssembly. The only download is the model file itself, which comes from Hugging Face and is cached locally.

Why does the first run take longer?

The model has to be downloaded once: about 26 MB for the Portrait model, 115 MB (WebGPU) or 224 MB (WebAssembly) for the Any-object model. Your browser caches it, so later images start instantly.

Which model should I pick?

Portrait (MODNet) is tuned for people — hair, shoulders, selfies — and is small and fast. Any object (BiRefNet Lite) handles products, animals, cars, logos and complex scenes with cleaner edges, at the cost of a bigger download and more processing time.

Can I get a white or coloured background instead of transparent?

Yes. Open Settings and choose White, Black or a custom colour. Transparent results are saved as PNG; with a solid fill you still get a PNG so edges stay crisp.

What is the maximum image size?

Images up to 25 megapixels are accepted. Very large photos are processed at a reduced internal resolution and the mask is scaled back to the original size, so the download keeps your full dimensions.

Why does it say WebGPU is unavailable?

Some browsers (older Safari, Firefox on Linux) or locked-down GPUs do not expose WebGPU. The tool automatically falls back to WebAssembly, which gives the same result, just more slowly.
